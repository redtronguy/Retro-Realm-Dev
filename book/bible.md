# The Tidebound Count — story bible

Reference for writing the novel and, later, adapting it into the game.
A reimagining of Dumas's *The Count of Monte Cristo* (public domain) as an
original fantasy, with powers.

## Tone and voice

- Third person past tense. Mostly Edric's point of view, with occasional chapters
  from Valtor, Mira, Dorran and others where the plot needs them.
- Dumas's sweep and drama (betrayal, patience, grand reveals) with modern fantasy
  readability. Rich but clear prose. PG-13: violence and dark themes, nothing graphic.
- The theme is the line between justice and vengeance, and whether power
  taken from suffering turns you into the thing that hurt you.

## The world

**Valemont**, a kingdom on a long southern coast. Its ruler for 300 years has been **Aurelian, the Sun King**,
who does not age. The **Radiant Order**, his priest-knights, wield *sunfire*.

- **Solace:** the capital, a white-and-gold city on cliffs around the **Sunspire**, the King's tower.
  Its **mirror towers** flash coded messages across the kingdom by sunlight (the heliograph).
  The **Chamber of Lords** sits here.
- **Saltmere:** a southern harbour town of fishers, traders and smugglers. Edric's home.
  - **The Weir:** a marsh quarter of stilt-houses, home to the Weir-folk, Mira's people.
  - **The Three Anchors:** the tavern where the betrayal is plotted.
- **The Drowned Keep:** a prison fortress on a black rock called **the Teeth**, half a day's sail from Saltmere.
  Prisoners who die are sewn into sacks and thrown into the sea. Officially it holds traitors.
  Secretly it is the lock on the Deep's chains.
- **The Shattered Reach:** smugglers' waters of reefs and islets far to the west.
- **The Isle of Cristal:** a deserted island in the Reach, home to the ruined citadel of the Tidewardens and their sunken treasury.
- **The Sardh:** desert kingdoms to the east. Fenrick made his fortune there by betraying Emir Tariq of Qasr.
- **The Greyhills:** bandit country on the road to Solace, ruled by the outlaw Vasco Rook.

### The history nobody is taught

Three hundred years ago the sea had a god: **the Deep**, vast, old and neither kind nor cruel.
The **Tidewardens**, an order of sea-mages, kept the balance between land and sea.

Aurelian, a prince of the Radiant Order, bound the Deep in chains of sunfire and made it the
foundation of his immortality. He drinks its life through the chains. Then he wiped out the
Tidewardens in **the Drying** and rewrote history. Officially he defeated "the Drowned Heresy"
and was blessed with eternal life by the Sun.

Since then the sea has been dying slowly: thinner fish runs, salt-blight on the shore,
storms that come at the wrong time. People blame bad luck.

## Magic

**Sunfire:** the Radiant Order's power, light and burning heat. Strongest at noon. It drives
off sea-creatures and hurts anything touched by the Deep.

**Tidecraft:** the Tidewardens' art. All water is one water, the sea, rain, tears and blood, and
a trained mind can feel and move it. Faro teaches Edric the discipline (breath, stillness, sensing),
but with the Deep chained, Tidecraft is only a trickle. Faro can make stone sweat and sense the weather.

**The Tidemark:** after Edric drowns and the Deep claims him, glowing sea-blue lines spread from his
heart. He becomes a channel for the chained god. Tidemark powers are strongest at night and at high
tide, and weakest at noon in direct sun.

Three disciplines, named by Faro long before Edric can use them:

1. **Riptide (force):** moving water and himself. Strength, speed, water blades, waves, pulling a
   ship through a storm. Combat.
2. **Undertow (pull):** sensing currents of every kind: the sea, the weather, and also the flow of
   want, debt and fear between people and of gold through a city. He can draw things down: sink a
   ship, drain a well, drag a man under. Trade, cunning and economic war.
3. **Still Water (reflection):** making his face a reflection, so others see what they expect.
   Disguise, seeing lies, scrying in water, and a deathlike sleep he can grant or take away.

**The cost, the Tide:** every use lets more of the Deep into him. He gets colder and tastes salt.
Memories not his own surface, he feels the god's three centuries of rage, and the Mark spreads up
his throat and face. Past a point he stops feeling pity. The Deep wants revenge and a body to carry
it. Edric's arc is about whether he masters the Tide or becomes it.

## Characters

### Edric Vane
19 at the start, 33 at the escape, 38 in Solace. A dockhand's son and gifted sailor, honest and warm,
and too trusting. Prison makes him patient, educated and cold. He takes the name **the Count of Cristal**.

His disguises:
- **Brother Faro:** a travelling priest, an homage to his teacher.
- **Master Wilde:** an eccentric foreign shipping agent.
- **"The Tide Remembers":** the anonymous signature on his gifts and warnings.

### The betrayers
| Who | Then | Now (Solace, 19 years later) | Sin | Falls by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dorran Quell** | Purser of the *Gull's Promise* | Baron Quell, banker and **Lord Treasurer** | Envy; writes the denunciation left-handed | Undertow: economic ruin, then mercy when he repents |
| **Fenrick Morrow** | Soldier, Mira's cousin | Count Morrow, **Lord Marshal** | Desire; posts the letter. Later betrays Emir Tariq at Qasr and sells his wife and daughter Hayda into slavery | Riptide and allies: Hayda testifies before the Chamber; he falls on his own sword |
| **Corvan Valtor** | Deputy magistrate in Saltmere | **High Justicar** of Valemont | Ambition; buries Edric to protect himself. Years later buries his own newborn son alive | Still Water: secrets exposed at the Pale Knight's trial; his wife kills herself and their son; he goes mad |
| **Cade Rusk** | Neighbour, failing tailor, drunk | Keeps a poor roadside inn | Cowardice: stays silent. Later greed: murders for Edric's diamond | His own greed: killed by the Pale Knight while burgling the Count's house |

### Edric's people
- **Tomas Vane:** Edric's father, an old sailor. Starves to death while Edric is in prison.
- **Mira Solen:** a Weir-folk fisher-girl, fierce and loyal, Edric's betrothed. After years believing
  him dead she marries Fenrick to save her mother from destitution. As Countess Morrow she has a son,
  Aren. She recognises Edric the moment she sees him.
- **Aren Morrow:** Mira and Fenrick's son, 16. Proud, decent and impulsive. Edric "rescues" him from
  Rook's bandits to enter Solace society. Aren later challenges the Count to a duel, then publicly apologises.
- **Master Faro:** the last Tidewarden, about 70, imprisoned for 30 years. Wry, brilliant and stubborn.
  Teaches Edric everything, deduces the betrayal, reveals the Lock and the treasure. Dies in his arms.
- **Pieter Moren:** the shipowner of the *Gull's Promise*, who tried to get Edric freed. Saved from
  ruin by the Count in secret.
  - **Maxim Moren:** Pieter's son, a young officer, in love with Lyse Valtor.
  - **Julie Moren:** Pieter's daughter.
- **Jacoba "Jac" Teague:** the smuggler captain who pulls Edric from the sea. Blunt, funny, loyal.
  Becomes his captain and right hand.
- **Hayda:** daughter of Emir Tariq of Qasr. Sold into slavery at 6; Edric buys her freedom at 17.
  She becomes his ward, then his truest companion. She testifies against Fenrick.
- **Bastien Corso:** Edric's steward, a former smuggler. Valtor's injustice killed his brother.
  Bastien once tried to stab Valtor in a garden and saw him bury a newborn alive. He dug up the baby,
  and his sister-in-law raised the boy, who grew up cruel and ran away.
- **Vasco Rook:** the outlaw king of the Greyhills. Owes Edric his life and lends him men.

### The Valtor household
- **Severin Valtor:** Corvan's father, once a leader of **the Ebb**, the secret movement to free the
  sea. Now paralysed and mute; he speaks by blinking. Sharp, furious, and protective of his granddaughter.
  The letter Edric carried was addressed to him.
- **Heloise Valtor:** Corvan's second wife. Poisons her way through the household so her son will
  inherit everything.
- **Pip Valtor:** their son, 9. Innocent. His death is Edric's breaking point.
- **Lyse Valtor:** Corvan's daughter by his first wife, 19. Gentle and brave; loves Maxim Moren.
  Edric saves her by faking her death with Still Water.
- **The Pale Knight (Andric):** Corvan's buried-alive son, 23. Handsome, charming, utterly without
  conscience. Edric dresses him up as the imposter **"Lord Andric Cavell"** and lets him court Dorran's
  daughter. Tried for Cade's murder, he reveals in court that the High Justicar is his father.

### The Quell household
- **Elsabet Quell:** Dorran's daughter. Sharp, a musician, wants nothing to do with marriage.
  Runs away the night the imposter is exposed.

### The great power
- **Aurelian, the Sun King:** golden, ageless and gracious; underneath, a 300-year-old will that
  has forgotten how to fear. He notices the Count late. When he does, he comes himself.
- **High Radiant Cassia Vell:** commander of the Radiant Order. Devout, and slowly realising the truth.

## Timeline

- **Year 0:** Edric (19) is betrayed and sent to the Drowned Keep. That same year the Ebb's rising
  is crushed in the north.
- **Year 2:** Faro breaks into his cell.
- **Year 13:** Faro dies. Edric escapes and becomes Tidebound.
- **Year 14:** he finds the treasure, visits Cade disguised, saves Moren, and begins building Cristal.
- **Years 14–19:** Cristal grows. He frees Hayda, gathers Bastien, Jac and Rook, and lays his plans.
  Fenrick, Dorran and Valtor rise to become the King's Three Hands.
- **Year 19:** Solace. The Hands fall, the King moves against Cristal, and the climax comes at the Drowned Keep.

## Ending (the novel's canon)

Edric does not free the Deep in rage. He takes the god's power into himself to break the chains, then
gives it back to the sea. The Deep is freed, but not loosed on the land. Without the stolen life,
Aurelian ages 300 years in moments and turns to dust. Edric loses the Tidemark and most of his powers.

He gives Cristal to its people and reunites Lyse (alive) with Maxim. He parts with Mira in peace and
sails west with Hayda. His last line: *"Wait, and hope."*

The game can branch: the Flood ending, the Tidewarden ending, and a secret ending.
